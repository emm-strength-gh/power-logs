Lilliebridge / 14-Week

Eric Lilliebridge's squat and deadlift block, weeks 1–14. Three sessions a week: a squat volume day, a secondary paused/deficit day, and a heavy day that alternates squat and deadlift week to week. The top set climbs through AMRAP waves (87 → 90 → 92 → 94%) into heavy singles (96 → 98%), then tapers at 92% before the week 14 deload. Enter both 1RMs and every set's weight is scaled from its programmed percentage, rounded to 2.5kg. In the weeks whose heavy pull falls on day 4 the lifter otherwise deadlifts only once, so a light speed pull is added to day 1 for frequency — dynamic-effort doubles at 55–65%, placed after the squat volume and scaled down as the day-4 top set climbs. Accessory work runs after every squat and deadlift day through week 11, then drops away so the heavy singles, taper and deload stay clean. SBD / Fatigue Managed

A recovery and rebuild block, four days a week — Monday squat+bench, Tuesday deadlift+bench, Thursday squat+bench, Saturday bench plus light posterior chain. Fatigue dissipates at RPE 5–6, then rebuilds behind RPE-led top singles, then reintroduces strength to test whether the fatigue has actually cleared. Repeat weeks are deliberate — loads hold unless demonstrated RPE says otherwise, and the rule travels with each week in the notes. Top singles carry no percentage on purpose: they are led by feel, so their weight column is blank.

Choose the path by evidence, not diagnosis. Functional and non-functional overreaching are only separable in hindsight — the difference is how long recovery takes — so the selector asks how long the decline has run instead. The paths are not a one-way fork: each has decision points that can move you between them, and those are generated into the notes. A prolonged decline should also be screened medically in week 1 rather than after the block, since a months-long drop has a real chance of a non-training cause.

Both paths carry a canary set — squat and bench at one fixed load every Monday for the whole block. Everything else moves, so its RPE trend is the only objective read on whether fatigue is clearing. Log it alongside waking heart rate, sleep, bodyweight and appetite. Enter your current working maxes, not your old PRs. Alongside the full program and the Clean export, each competition lift can be exported on its own — and those solo exports are percentage-free too, so only the full Program view shows the maths.

Conjugate + VBT 10-Week

A four-day upper/lower split running max-effort, dynamic-effort and repetition-effort work concurrently — Monday ME Lower, Tuesday DE Upper, Thursday DE Lower, Friday ME Upper. Max-effort variations rotate on a two-week cadence from a deliberately small, purpose-matched pool, then give way to the competition lifts: variations dominate weeks 1–3, sit close to the comp lifts in weeks 5–7, and vanish entirely from week 8. Dynamic-effort loads wave 55% → 77.5% rather than sitting in the classic speed band, because below roughly 50–65% a barbell squat or bench spends much of the concentric decelerating.

Three loading currencies run side by side. RPE governs max-effort top sets and heavy supplemental work, where it is most reliable. Percentages govern dynamic-effort work, where RPE is least reliable. Velocity targets (MCV) and velocity-loss stop rules govern volume — VL 10% cancels the rest of a DE exercise outright, 20% ends a backoff set, 25% ends an accessory set. When signals conflict the order is pain, then velocity loss, then the weekly RPE cap, then the written prescription.

Loads key off Training Maxes, not aspirational numbers: a 1RM from the last eight weeks, or an RPE-9 single divided by 0.955. Err low. Max-effort top sets are prescribed against the max of a variation, so those rows are scaled from the source's variation-to-comp-lift table and flagged (est.) — replace them once you have real numbers. Velocity figures are population anchors; build your own profile in week 0 and re-run it in week 4.

Massthetics / Deadlift Only 3.0

An eight-week deadlift specialisation block, three days a week, two movements per day. Day 1 is the heavy competition-stance pull with a secondary pull behind it — hamstring work in week 1, snatch grip from week 2. Day 2 pairs the competition-stance volume pull (ten sets) with front squats. Day 3 is paused high-bar squats and deficit pulls. The heavy pull climbs 80 → 85 → 90 → 95% while the volume pull waves 75 → 85%, so week 6 is the heaviest week of the source material.

Percentages resolve against a training max, not a true 1RM — the source's own volume proves it. Ten sets of three at 85% and six doubles at 95% are impossible against a true max (that is 30 reps at 85% and 12 at 95%), but against a 90% TM they land at 76% and 86% of true 1RM, which is coherent. The same reading fixes the squat work: 70% of a squat TM is about 77% of a front squat max — a real accessory prescription — where 70% of a true back squat would be roughly 86% of a front squat for sets of five. Every percentage therefore references the competition lift: deadlift TM for all four pulls, squat TM for both squats.

Weeks 7 and 8 are additions, not in the Massthetics source, and are labelled as such in the notes. Week 7 is a deload: deadlift volume drops about 54% against week 6 (74 → 34 reps) and every pull sits at 50–60% of your true 1RM. Week 8 is day 1 rest, day 2 max out — warm-up ladder, an opener that should feel easy, a second attempt at your old best, then PR attempts above 100%. Deload and max-out loads resolve against the 1RM rather than the TM, because a deload and a test are anchored to real capacity.
